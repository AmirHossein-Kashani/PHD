## Literature Review: Building a Stance-Aware, Knowledge-Graph-Grounded, Multi-Document Medical Fact-Verification System

### TL;DR
- The individual building blocks you need already exist and are mostly mature: dynamic KG construction from text (**Microsoft GraphRAG**, **MedGraphRAG**, **REBEL**), biomedical NER/normalization (**BERN2**, **SciSpacy**, UMLS/SNOMED/MeSH), biomedical retrieval (**MedCPT**, **SPECTER2**, **BM25 hybrid**), and scientific claim verification with support/refute labels (**SciFact**, **HealthVer**, **MultiVerS**). Your genuine novelty is the *combination*: a two-sided (support vs. contradict) evidence split built on a dynamically-constructed medical KG, with a final consolidated verdict kept separate from the two evidence sides and an evidence-balance-derived confidence score.
- No existing system does all of this. The closest precedents are **MedGraphRAG** (ACL 2025, graph-grounded evidence-based medical generation), the "Topic-Aware Evidence Reasoning and Stance-Aware Aggregation" line (Si et al., ACL 2021), and CONFACT (resolving conflicting evidence with source-credibility weighting, IJCAI 2025). Each solves one piece; none produces a stance-split, KG-grounded, confidence-scored verdict.
- The hardest and least-solved part is calibrated confidence derived from the *balance and strength* of conflicting evidence. LLM verbalized confidence is systematically overconfident (Xiong et al., ICLR 2024 find verbalized confidence values "predominantly fall within the 80% to 100% range and are typically in multiples of 5"); conformal prediction offers distribution-free guarantees but is not yet standard for two-sided evidence aggregation. This is where you should concentrate research effort and where clinical-deployment risk is highest.

### Key Findings
1. **MindMap and its lineage are a strong conceptual template but assume a *pre-existing* KG.** MindMap (Wen, Wang, Sun, ACL 2024) builds path-based and neighbor-based evidence subgraphs from a curated KG, merges them into reasoning graphs, and prompts the LLM to produce a "mind map" with traceable reasoning. Your extension — building the KG on-the-fly from retrieved papers — is closer to GraphRAG/MedGraphRAG than to MindMap's original design.
1. **Two-sided evidence is natively supported by the fact-checking literature, not the KG-RAG literature.** SciFact, HealthVer, and MultiVerS all label evidence as SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NEI with rationale sentences. This is the natural backbone for your stance split. The KG layer should *organize and explain* this evidence, not replace the stance classifier.
1. **Confidence from evidence balance is under-researched and is your riskiest novel component.** Most calibration work derives confidence from model internals or self-report, not from an explicit tally of supporting vs. contradicting evidence weighted by source quality. You will likely need to design this and validate it empirically.

### Details

#### Area 1 — KG-grounded LLM reasoning and prompting
**MindMap (Wen, Wang, Sun, ACL 2024; arXiv 2308.09729).** The pipeline has three steps: (i) evidence-graph construction from a source KG, producing both *path-based* subgraphs (entity-to-entity reasoning chains) and *neighbor-based* subgraphs (1-hop context around query entities); (ii) aggregation of these into merged reasoning graphs; (iii) LLM "graph-of-thought" prompting (built with the LangChain technique) that asks the model to build a mind map, perform synergistic inference combining KG knowledge with its parametric knowledge, and index the knowledge sources used. It was evaluated mainly in the medical domain (e.g., the ChatDoctor/CMCKG datasets) and introduced a hallucination evaluation benchmark. Its key limitation for you: it depends on a curated KG existing up front and traverses it; it does not extract a KG from retrieved literature, and it has no notion of splitting evidence into support vs. contradict.
**Think-on-Graph (Sun et al., ICLR 2024; arXiv 2307.07697).** Implements the "LLM ⊗ KG" paradigm: the LLM acts as an agent that performs iterative beam search on a KG, alternating "explore" (find candidate triples) and "think" (decide which triples are relevant) steps until enough evidence is gathered. Strengths: deep multi-hop reasoning, knowledge traceability and correctability, plug-and-play with any KG/LLM. Limitation: assumes a high-quality static KG to walk over.
**Graph of Thoughts (Besta et al., AAAI 2024; arXiv 2308.09687).** Models reasoning as an arbitrary graph where vertices are "LLM thoughts" and edges are dependencies. Its three core transformations are *aggregation/merging* (combining multiple thoughts into a synergistic outcome), *refinement/looping* (feedback loops that iteratively improve a thought), and *generation* (spawning new thoughts). The paper reports increasing the quality of a sorting task by 62% over Tree-of-Thoughts while simultaneously reducing cost by >31% (note: this headline figure is specific to the sorting task, not a universal average). For your design, GoT is the right abstraction for the *aggregation* step where two evidence subgraphs are merged into a verdict. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09687)
**Microsoft GraphRAG (2024).** Constructs a KG from an unstructured corpus *autonomously* using an LLM: text-unit segmentation → iterative entity/relation extraction with GPT-4 → community detection via the Leiden algorithm → hierarchical community summaries used to augment prompts at query time. This is the closest match to your requirement to "build a KG dynamically from retrieved documents." Important caveats: indexing is very expensive (Microsoft documents it as 100–1000× the cost of vector RAG; LazyGraphRAG reduces indexing cost to ~0.1% of full GraphRAG); and the global community-summary approach is optimized for thematic "global" questions, not stance classification. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05264)[Articsledge](https://www.articsledge.com/post/graphrag-retrieval-augmented-generation)
**KG-RAG (Soman et al., Bioinformatics 2024; arXiv 2311.17330).** A biomedical framework using the SPOKE KG (over 27 million nodes of 21 types, ~53 million edges of 55 types per the project repo) with Llama-2-13b, GPT-3.5, GPT-4. Steps: entity recognition from the prompt → extract biomedical concepts from SPOKE → concept embedding → prompt-aware (minimal sufficient) context generation → conversion to natural language → answer with provenance. It reported a 71% performance boost for Llama-2 on a curated MCQ set and >50% token reduction versus naïve graph context. It is "specifically designed for running prompts related to Diseases" per its repository — directly aligned with your disease-query focus — but uses a curated KG, not one built from retrieved papers. [arXiv + 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17330)
**MedGraphRAG (Wu et al., ACL 2025; arXiv 2408.04187).** The most directly relevant system. It introduces *Triple Graph Construction* (segment documents → extract entities → build a three-level hierarchical graph linking user documents to credible medical sources up to foundational medical knowledge, forming meta-graphs merged by similarity into a global graph) and *U-Retrieval* (top-down precise retrieval + bottom-up response refinement). Per a 2025 Springer survey (Neural Computing & Applications) summarizing the work, MedGraphRAG "achieves 8% improvement over standard RAG in medical QA and 10% in fact-checking tasks through graph-based retrieval," validated "on 9 medical Q&A benchmarks, 2 health fact-checking datasets, and a long-form generation test set," and emphasizes evidence-grounded responses with citations. It does *not* split evidence into support vs. contradict, nor produce a confidence score — these are your additions. [arXiv + 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04187v1)
**Pan et al., "Unifying LLMs and Knowledge Graphs: A Roadmap" (IEEE TKDE 2024, 36(7):3580–3599; arXiv 2306.08302).** The canonical survey. Defines three integration patterns: KG-enhanced LLMs, LLM-augmented KGs, and Synergized LLMs+KGs. Your system spans all three: you use an LLM to *build* the KG (LLM-augmented KG), then use the KG to *ground* reasoning (KG-enhanced LLM), in a closed loop (synergized). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.08302v3)

#### Area 2 — KG construction from unstructured medical text
**Biomedical NER + normalization.** BERN2 (Sung et al., Bioinformatics 2022; arXiv 2201.02080) is a multi-task neural NER+NEN tool that recognizes nine biomedical entity types from text or a PMID and normalizes each concept to standard identifiers (NCBI Gene, ChEBI, MeSH, OMIM, etc.) via a sieve-based approach; the authors explicitly recommend it for "biomedical knowledge graph construction." SciSpacy provides lighter-weight NER models (e.g., en_ner_bionlp13cg_md) and UMLS entity linking; BioBERT and PubMedBERT are the standard fine-tuning backbones (BioBERT improves over BERT by ~0.62% F1 on NER and ~2.80% F1 on relation extraction per its original benchmarks). [PubMed + 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36053172/)
**Relation extraction / triple extraction.** REBEL (Babelscape, an autoregressive BART-based seq2seq model covering 200+ relation types, ~74 micro-F1 / ~51 macro-F1 on its benchmark) is the standard open-source joint entity-relation extractor and integrates with LlamaIndex. For biomedical RE specifically, BioREX and BERT-GT (PubMedBERT-based) are strong. LLM-based triple extraction (GPT-4/Claude prompted for closed or open triples) is increasingly competitive: studies show GPT-4 can match or exceed REBEL when given detailed instructions, at higher cost. CoDe-KG combines coreference resolution with syntactic decomposition and reports +8% F1 over prior methods on REBEL's dataset and +20% recall on rare relations. [Medium + 4](https://medium.com/@EleventhHourEnthusiast/paper-review-enhancing-knowledge-graph-construction-using-large-language-models-1981ec1c063b)
**Ontologies / normalization targets.** UMLS (the integrating metathesaurus), SNOMED CT (clinical terminology), MeSH (PubMed indexing), and RxNorm (drugs) are the normalization vocabularies. PrimeKG standardizes disease nodes to the MONDO ontology — a useful model for your entity-resolution layer. [Elucidata](https://www.elucidata.io/blog/evolving-from-primekg-to-polly-knowledge-graph)
**Curated medical KGs vs. on-the-fly construction.** PrimeKG (Chandak, Huang, Zitnik, Nature Scientific Data 2023) integrates 20 resources to describe 17,080 diseases with 4,050,249 relationships across ten biological scales; Hetionet integrates 29 databases; SPOKE has 27M+ nodes. These are excellent *background* KGs but are static and cannot reflect a freshly-retrieved paper's specific claims. Your design calls for dynamic construction from retrieved documents — but a hybrid is strongly advisable: build the dynamic KG from retrieved papers, then *link/normalize* its entities against PrimeKG/UMLS for canonical IDs and to detect when a retrieved claim contradicts established knowledge. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-01960-3)[Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/systems-biology/articles/10.3389/fsysb.2025.1651930/full)

#### Area 3 — Multi-document biomedical retrieval
**MedCPT (Jin et al., Bioinformatics 2023, btad651; arXiv 2307.00589).** A contrastively pre-trained retriever+re-ranker: per the paper, the authors "collected an unprecedented scale of 255 million user click logs from PubMed... MedCPT sets new state-of-the-art performance on six biomedical IR tasks, outperforming various baselines including much larger models, such as GPT-3-sized cpt-text-XL" (175B). This should be your primary dense retriever for PubMed.
**SPECTER / SPECTER2 (Cohan et al., 2020; arXiv 2004.07180).** Document-level scientific embeddings trained on the citation graph; SPECTER2 trained on 6M+ citation triplets across 23 fields with task-specific adapters. Good for paper-level relatedness and recommendation. [arxiv + 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.07180)
**Contriever and BM25.** Contriever (Izacard et al. 2022) is a general-domain dense retriever; BM25 remains a strong lexical baseline. Hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense, fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, then a cross-encoder re-ranker) is the consensus best practice. [arxiv + 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13178)
**MedRAG / MIRAGE (Xiong et al., Findings of ACL 2024; arXiv 2402.13178).** The key benchmark. MedRAG indexes PubMed (23.9M articles), StatPearls (9.3k articles / 301.2k snippets), 18 medical Textbooks, and Wikipedia — combined as "MedCorp" (30.4M docs / 54.2M snippets). It compares BM25, Contriever, SPECTER, and MedCPT plus RRF fusion (default 32 snippets). MIRAGE comprises 7,663 questions from five datasets (MMLU-Med, MedQA-US, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, BioASQ-Y/N). Per the paper, "MedRAG improves the accuracy of six different LLMs by up to 18% over chain-of-thought prompting, elevating the performance of GPT-3.5 and Mixtral to GPT-4-level"; experiments used "over 1.8 trillion prompt tokens on 41 combinations," and GPT-3.5 "benefits the most from MedRag (+17.9%)." Two findings matter for you: a *log-linear* relationship between performance and number of retrieved snippets (with degradation when k is too large as signal-to-noise drops), and a *"lost-in-the-middle"* U-shaped effect where accuracy is lowest when the ground-truth snippet sits in the middle of the context. The paper explicitly names claim verification as a future MedRAG application. [arxiv + 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13178)

#### Area 4 — Stance detection / claim verification (the core)
**SciFact (Wadden et al., EMNLP 2020; aclanthology 2020.emnlp-main.609).** A corpus with 5,183 relevant abstracts for 1,409 expert-written scientific claims; each (claim, abstract) pair is labeled SUPPORTS / REFUTES / NOINFO with annotated rationale sentences (no abstract has more than three rationales for a given claim). The canonical task definition for your two-sided split. SciFact-Open (2022) extends the corpus to 500K abstracts for open-domain retrieval. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372933925_SciFact-Open_Towards_open-domain_scientific_claim_verification)
**HealthVer (Sarrouti et al., Findings of EMNLP 2021).** 14,330 evidence–claim pairs (1,855 claims) for COVID/health claims verified against CORD-19 abstracts, labeled SUPPORTS / REFUTES / NEUTRAL — real-world (not synthetic) claims from search queries. The most relevant dataset for health-claim verification. [ResearchGate + 5](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Distribution-of-Complex-Claims-in-HEALTHVER-FEVER-PUBHEALTH-and-SCIFACT-according-to_fig3_357384460)
**PubHealth (Kotonya & Toni, 2020).** Public-health claims with True/False/Mixture/Unproven labels plus journalist-written gold explanations — useful for the explanation/consolidation component. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20876)
**COVID-Fact (Saakyan et al., 2021).** 4,086 claims from Reddit with supporting/refuting evidence, including automatically generated claim negations. FEVER (185,445 claims) and its variants are the large general-domain backbone for pretraining/transfer. [arxiv + 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18670)
**MultiVerS / LongChecker (Wadden et al., Findings of NAACL 2022; arXiv 2112.01640).** Predicts a document-level SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NEI label *and* selects rationale sentences in a multitask fashion using a Longformer encoding of the claim plus full-document context. It supports weak supervision and strong zero/few-shot domain adaptation — important because you will have little labeled in-domain data. This is the best off-the-shelf model to adapt for per-document stance labeling. [Emergent Mind + 2](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/multivers)
**Biomedical NLI for sentence-level stance.** MedNLI (Romanov & Shivade, EMNLP 2018) provides 14,049 clinical premise–hypothesis pairs (11,232 train / 1,395 dev / 1,422 test) labeled entailment/contradiction/neutral, drawn from MIMIC-III; inter-annotator kappa 0.78. SOTA fine-tuned models reach ~83% accuracy (BioBERT PMC+PubMed fine-tuned on MIMIC-III achieves 83.45% per the Saama Research/MEDIQA 2019 system; PubMedBERT ~0.83; SciFive and Clinical-T5 are competitive, with Lehman et al. 2023 reporting Clinical-T5 surpassing SciFive on MedNLI). The 3-label entailment/contradiction/neutral scheme maps cleanly onto your support/contradict/neutral split. SciNLI (Sadat & Caragea, ACL 2022, 107,412 pairs) uses a 4-class scheme (entailment/reasoning/contrasting/neutral, best reported Macro-F1 ~78%) that does *not* map 1:1 and would need relabeling. [arxiv + 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.00160)
**Stance-aware aggregation.** "Topic-Aware Evidence Reasoning and Stance-Aware Aggregation for Fact Verification" (Si et al., ACL 2021) explicitly aggregates evidence pieces by stance — directly relevant to your design.

#### Area 5 — Evidence aggregation across conflicting sources
**CONFACT (Resolving Conflicting Evidence in Automated Fact-Checking; IJCAI 2025; arXiv 2505.17762).** Introduces a dataset where retrieved documents take opposing stances, annotated with source credibility (5-point scale) and source category (Mainstream News, Government, Non-profit, Academic, Social Media, Other). It tests three strategies for incorporating source credibility: Source Filtering, Credibility Weighting in ranking, and Source Background Augmentation. Key finding: vanilla RAG LLMs are poor at reasoning through conflicting evidence and at prioritizing trustworthy sources; injecting source metadata helps. This is the most direct precedent for your evidence-weighting layer. [arXivarXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17762v1)
**"Contradiction to Consensus" (arXiv 2602.18693).** A dual-perspective, multi-source (Wikipedia + PubMed + Google) retrieval framework that deduplicates, merges, and ranks sentences to form a unified evidence set per claim, explicitly capturing both supporting and contradicting evidence that any single source might miss, with source-level disagreement modeling. It notes PubMed is strongest for specialized biomedical claims while Wikipedia serves everyday health queries. Conceptually the closest published work to your two-sided design.
**GEAR (Graph-based Evidence Aggregating and Reasoning).** An earlier FEVER-era graph approach to aggregating multiple evidence pieces; relevant as a graph-aggregation precedent.
**Aggregation strategy options for your verdict layer.** (a) Simple voting over per-document stance labels; (b) weighted scoring by source quality (study type — RCT > cohort > case report; recency; journal; sample size); (c) Bayesian aggregation treating each piece of evidence as updating a prior on claim veracity; (d) LLM-based synthesis prompted with the two evidence sets. Evidence-based-medicine hierarchies (systematic reviews/meta-analyses > RCTs > observational > expert opinion) give you a principled, clinically-recognized weighting scheme that should be encoded explicitly rather than left to the LLM.

#### Area 6 — Confidence estimation and calibration
**Verbalized confidence is overconfident.** Xiong et al. (ICLR 2024, "Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty?", arXiv 2306.13063) benchmark prompting + sampling + aggregation methods across GPT-4 and LLaMA 2 and find that "LLMs, when verbalizing their confidence, tend to be overconfident, potentially imitating human patterns," with verbalized confidence values "predominantly fall[ing] within the 80% to 100% range and... typically in multiples of 5." Tian et al. (2023) find RLHF-tuned models' verbalized confidence can be better-calibrated than raw token probabilities. Verbalized confidence is cheap, model-agnostic, and works with closed APIs, but must be treated as a weak signal and recalibrated. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07767)[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14737v2)
**Conformal prediction provides distribution-free guarantees.** "Language Models with Conformal Factuality Guarantees" (Mohri & Hashimoto, ICML 2024; arXiv 2402.10978) connects factuality to conformal prediction, providing high-probability correctness by progressively removing low-confidence sub-claims; evaluations on FActScore, NaturalQuestions and MATH "show that our approach can provide 80-90% correctness guarantees while retaining the majority of the LM's original output." Conformal methods need a labeled calibration set but give rigorous, tunable coverage — attractive for a clinical setting where you can specify an acceptable error rate.
**Confidence from evidence balance — the gap.** The "Double-Calibration" line (arXiv 2601.11956) argues that confidence derived solely from the model's internal state is vulnerable to knowledge gaps and overconfidence and proposes anchoring confidence to an external source (knowledge + reasoning). This validates your intuition: confidence should be derived from the *external evidence balance* (count and strength of supporting vs. contradicting, weighted by source quality), not from the LLM's self-report. There is no standard method for this — you will design it. A defensible formulation: compute a signed, source-quality-weighted evidence score; map its magnitude to confidence and its sign to the verdict; calibrate the mapping on a held-out labeled set (HealthVer/SciFact) and optionally wrap it in conformal prediction for a coverage guarantee.

#### Area 7 — Implementation and clinical deployment
**Frameworks.** LangChain (used by MindMap for its graph-of-thought prompting; offers Neo4jGraph integration) and LlamaIndex (integrates REBEL for triple extraction and has a Knowledge Graph Query Engine) are the natural orchestration layers. Neo4j is the standard graph store (Cypher queries; MindMap and several biomedical QA systems use it; note Cypher-RAG over very large graphs like SPOKE needs large context windows). For retrieval, FAISS/Milvus vector stores plus MedCPT/SPECTER2 embeddings; Pyserini for BM25. [arxiv + 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09729)
**Evaluation.** Standard stack: F1/precision/recall on SUPPORTS/REFUTES/NEI labels and rationale selection (SciFact/HealthVer leaderboards); BERTScore and GPT-4-as-a-judge for generated explanations; calibration metrics (ECE, reliability diagrams) and conformal coverage for the confidence score; and a hallucination benchmark in the style MindMap introduced. MIRAGE (7,663 questions) gives you a ready medical QA evaluation harness.
**Regulation and safety.** A medical fact-verification tool that informs clinical decisions may be regulated as Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) or fall under Clinical Decision Support (CDS) carve-outs. Under the US 21st Century Cures Act, CDS software that *supports rather than replaces* clinician judgment and allows the clinician to independently review the basis of the recommendation can be exempt from device regulation — which makes your *traceability/explainability* design (evidence subgraphs + cited sources + the two-sided split) not just a feature but a regulatory enabler. Recent FDA commentary (2026) stresses that where models are opaque (black-box LLMs), time-critical, or directive, device oversight should still be expected. FDA's Good Machine Learning Practice principles emphasize transparency, bias mitigation, and post-market monitoring (data drift). Bias in medical KGs and training corpora (under-representation of certain populations) is a documented risk you must audit. A recent conceptual framework (PMC, "auditable and source-verified framework for clinical AI decision support") proposes exactly your ingredients: a provenance-tagged knowledge base, a RAG engine that links recommendations to identifiable peer-reviewed sources, and tamper-evident audit logging — a good blueprint for regulatory readiness. [Aihealthcarecompliance](https://aihealthcarecompliance.com/resources/applicable-laws/fda-ai-ml/)

### Recommendations
**Proposed pipeline architecture (mapping methods to components):**
1. **Query intake & claim formalization.** Normalize the disease/claim into a checkable proposition; extract query entities with BERN2/SciSpacy and link to UMLS/MONDO IDs.
1. **Multi-document retrieval.** Hybrid retrieval over PubMed: BM25 + MedCPT dense, fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, re-ranked by the MedCPT cross-encoder. Retrieve ~16–32 abstracts/full texts (heed the log-linear and lost-in-the-middle findings — re-rank so the strongest evidence is not buried mid-context). English-only filter.
1. **Dynamic KG construction.** Per retrieved document, run NER+normalization (BERN2) and relation/triple extraction (REBEL and/or LLM-based extraction with a constrained biomedical relation schema). Normalize entities to UMLS/MONDO and *link against PrimeKG/SPOKE* for canonical IDs and to flag agreement/conflict with established knowledge. Store in Neo4j with provenance (PMID, sentence offsets) on every edge.
1. **Stance classification (the core).** For each document/rationale, classify stance toward the claim with a MultiVerS-style model and/or a biomedical NLI model (PubMedBERT/SciFive fine-tuned on MedNLI + HealthVer) producing SUPPORTS / CONTRADICTS / NEUTRAL with rationale sentences. Partition evidence into the two sides; attach each piece to its KG subgraph.
1. **Two-sided evidence subgraphs.** Build a support subgraph and a contradict subgraph (MindMap-style path + neighbor construction, but over your dynamic KG), each traceable to source sentences.
1. **Consolidated verdict (kept separate).** Use a GoT-style aggregation step: prompt the LLM with both subgraphs to synthesize a verdict, but compute the verdict label primarily from an explicit, EBM-weighted evidence tally (study type, recency, sample size, source credibility à la CONFACT) so the conclusion is auditable and not solely LLM-generated.
1. **Confidence scoring.** Compute a signed, source-quality-weighted net-evidence score; map magnitude→confidence, sign→verdict; calibrate on held-out HealthVer/SciFact; optionally wrap in conformal prediction for a coverage guarantee. Report confidence alongside the two evidence sides and the verdict, never merged into them.

**Staged plan.**
- *Stage 1 (reuse, ~weeks):* Stand up hybrid PubMed retrieval (MedCPT + BM25) and a MultiVerS/NLI stance classifier; evaluate on SciFact + HealthVer. Benchmark on MIRAGE.
- *Stage 2 (integrate):* Add dynamic KG construction (REBEL/BERN2 + Neo4j) and the two-sided subgraph organization; compare against MedGraphRAG as a baseline.
- *Stage 3 (novelty):* Build and calibrate the evidence-balance confidence module; run calibration (ECE) and conformal coverage experiments.
- *Stage 4 (clinical/regulatory):* Add provenance logging, bias auditing, and a clinician-in-the-loop review UI; design as CDS (clinician can inspect the two-sided evidence) to support a non-device or low-risk regulatory posture.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that should change your approach.** If stance-classification F1 on HealthVer is below ~0.7, prioritize NLI fine-tuning before investing in the KG layer. If the KG layer does not improve verdict accuracy or explanation quality over a strong text-only RAG baseline (it may not, given GraphRAG's cost), keep the KG only for explanation/traceability rather than as the primary reasoning substrate. If confidence ECE stays high (>~0.1) after recalibration, adopt conformal prediction and surface abstention ("insufficient evidence") rather than a numeric score.

### Caveats
- **Cost.** LLM-based dynamic KG construction per query is expensive (GraphRAG indexing is 100–1000× vector RAG); LazyGraphRAG-style lazy construction or restricting extraction to top-ranked sentences is advisable.
- **Stance label scarcity in your exact setting.** SciFact/HealthVer claims are not identical to free-form disease queries; expect a domain gap and rely on MultiVerS-style weak supervision and domain adaptation.
- **The confidence module is genuinely novel and unvalidated.** Treat its design as a research contribution requiring its own evaluation; do not present its scores as clinically validated without calibration evidence.
- **KG/corpus bias.** Curated KGs and PubMed over-represent some populations/conditions; audit for this, especially before any clinical use.
- **Some cited figures are task- or system-specific** (e.g., GoT's "+62% / >31%" is for sorting; KG-RAG's "71% boost" is for one MCQ set; MedGraphRAG's ~8–10% gains are on its chosen benchmarks; MedRAG's "+17.9%" is GPT-3.5 specifically) and may not transfer to your setting.
- **Regulatory status is fact-specific and evolving;** the CDS exemption depends on the clinician being able to independently review the basis of each output and on the tool not being directive or time-critical. Obtain regulatory counsel before deployment; recent (2026) FDA guidance signals continued scrutiny of opaque LLM-based tools.
- **A few supporting sources are secondary** (Medium/vendor posts, ResearchGate mirrors, a Springer survey summarizing MedGraphRAG's numbers) used only for descriptive context; all core claims trace to peer-reviewed papers or arXiv primary sources.
