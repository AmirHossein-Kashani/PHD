# Biology and Chemistry Background Plan

> [!summary] In short
> Week-by-week plan to learn enough chemistry, biology and drug discovery basics.

**Topics:** [[Biomedicine and Drug Discovery]], [[Skills and Tools]]  
**Notion category:** [[Notion Category - Documentation]]  
**Original:** [Notion page](https://app.notion.com/p/3cad68da32f0805299eadf0b3f9a26be)

---

**Week 1 — Chemistry & biology, just the map**
- [ ] Watch **Professor Dave Explains**' organic chemistry playlist at 1.5× — you want to recognize functional groups, structures, and stereochemistry, not solve mechanisms. (~5–6 hrs)
- [ ] Watch a **Ninja Nerd** or **Armando Hasudungan** intro to proteins, enzymes, and receptors — enough to know what a "drug target" is. (~3–4 hrs)
- [ ] Don't take notes on reactions. Just build a mental picture.

**Week 2 — The actual drug part (highest value)**
- [ ] This is the layer that matters most for familiarity. Read the *first two or three chapters only* of Graham Patrick's *Introduction to Medicinal Chemistry*, or find a lecture-series summary of it. Focus on: SAR, drug-likeness (Lipinski's rules), ADMET, and the discovery pipeline (target → hit → lead → optimization → trials).
- [ ] Skim a med-chem review article or two so you see the vocabulary in context.
- [ ] This week alone gets you 60% of the "sounds like I know the field" effect.

**Week 3 — Your home turf, made concrete**
- [ ] Run 3–4 of **Pat Walters' Practical Cheminformatics** notebooks in Google Colab — SMILES, fingerprints, similarity search. Since you code, an afternoon here makes all the abstract chemistry from Week 1 click.
- [ ] Skim the **TeachOpenCADD** talktorial titles/intros to see what computer-aided drug design actually involves (docking, virtual screening, ML models) — you don't need to complete them, just know they exist and roughly how they work.

**Optional Week 4 — Pharmacology gloss**
- [ ] A **Speed Pharmacology** intro video or two, just to know pharmacokinetics vs. pharmacodynamics. Low priority for pure familiarity.
